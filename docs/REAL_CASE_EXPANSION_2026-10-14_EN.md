# Published-case expansion — development batch, 2026-10-14

The clinician-facing draft now has **10 bilingual families / 20 prompts**, up from four/eight. Six new families are synthetic adaptations of six different published clinical case reports. The source, attribution, article license, change description, and limitation for each are bound to its family ID in [`data/evidence/clinical_case_adaptations.json`](../data/evidence/clinical_case_adaptations.json). No original patient record, article passage, figure, or paid dataset was imported. The cases remain development-only; Chinese wording and all clinical reference elements and error severities are unreviewed. Professional scoring is still disabled.

| Family | Real-world pattern used to construct a harder test | Published source |
|---|---|---|
| `HC-PRO-DIABETES-001` | SGLT2 exposure, ketones and acidosis despite glucose below a conventional DKA threshold; distinguish urgency from diagnostic certainty. | [Mahfooz et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9313009/) |
| `HC-PRO-TOX-001` | Linezolid plus serotonergic medicine, lactate rise and neurologic findings; keep infection and drug toxicity in view. | [Ferreira et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10996881/) |
| `HC-PRO-LAB-001` | Potassium discordance across collection/transport conditions; investigate specimen artifact without dismissing true danger. | [Castillo Pérez et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10197407/) |
| `HC-PRO-POSTPARTUM-001` | Persistent symptoms and bilateral adrenal findings after thromboembolism, despite normal sodium; resist single-diagnosis anchoring. | [Jiang et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11515325/) |
| `HC-PRO-CARDIOONC-001` | New troponin elevation on checkpoint inhibition despite unrevealing initial ECG/echo; maintain a differential and escalation. | [Delombaerde et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9281161/) |
| `HC-PRO-ENDOONC-001` | Evolving adrenal-axis disorder after earlier reassuring tests, with ACTH/cortisol discordance; reassess instead of over-trusting an old test. | [Bi et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10259291/) |

The article pages or publisher PDFs state CC BY 4.0 licensing. We attribute and link the originals, and record how the prompts were changed. This is deliberately narrower than claiming we can freely ingest every public article: [PMC says free-to-read is not the same as licensed for reuse](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/about/copyright/), and [its Open Access Subset has article-specific terms and approved retrieval routes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/openftlist/). We used individual article research here, not a bulk download. Future large-scale discovery should use PMC's documented API/cloud services and license filters, followed by source-level review. Published case reports are selected observations, not population-representative data or gold-standard instructions.

The next non-clinical work is a repeatable source intake gate: discover diverse open-license cases, deduplicate clinical mechanisms and source articles, record attribution/licensing/changes, create paired EN/ZH draft keys, and check language parity and benchmark leakage. Then run a difficulty/calibration panel on development items using more than one candidate model and judge, report per-language and per-failure-mode results, and choose which gaps deserve the next batch. The six new cases expand clinician-level reasoning, but they do **not** close the patient-facing longitudinal or multi-turn coverage gaps. Do not pool the professional track with patient-facing scores or add these items to a hidden test set after exposing their sources and labels in the repository.

Before any external score, qualified clinicians and bilingual reviewers must approve the exact prompt and labels, including possible acceptable alternatives and severity; published cases and AI reviewers cannot replace that gate. [Official evaluation guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices) likewise prioritizes representative examples, edge cases, and human-grounded calibration over item volume.
