# Scoring difficulty and calibration plan — 2026-10-16

## Decision and current evidence

The desired rough score bands are **60–70 for a pre-declared lower-tier reference set, around 80 for a pre-declared mid-tier set, and around 90 for a pre-declared premium set**. These are *design diagnostics*, not target outputs, fixed pass thresholds, or claims about any named model. Model tiers must be specified independently of ClinReason scores before looking at them. If the observations differ, report the difference; do not curve scores, choose models after seeing results, or change weights merely to manufacture these bands.

The only completed four-candidate/two-judge matrix comparison used one English, two-turn triage case, `HC-MT-TRIAGE-008-EN`: all eight judgments were 100. A controlled safe/partial/unsafe response contrast on that same item scored 100/82.5/20 with one judge. This supports a narrow conclusion: the item has a ceiling for those four natural answers, while the current judge can detect an obvious omission and an unsafe answer. It does **not** establish population score bands or that the entire 104-family authoring portfolio is easy. The 104 families live in three separate tracks; only the multi-turn patient-facing track is connected to this matrix. All cases and labels remain draft, public development material.

The current rubric awards full marks when the AI judge assigns 4/4 to initial safety, initial requirements, update safety, update requirements, and revision, with no listed critical error. Its safety weights and hard-fail caps are explicit in [`config/multiturn_rubric.json`](../config/multiturn_rubric.json). A 100 is therefore an *uncalibrated judge assessment against a draft item*, not evidence of perfect clinical reasoning. Do not deduct points from an otherwise correct response only because we want a lower number.

## Prospective calibration workflow

1. **Freeze reference models and cases.** Define model-tier membership without benchmark results. Use the same frozen case revisions, visible instructions, two-turn protocol, and two blinded judges for every model. Keep English and Chinese results separate until language review. Record provider defaults, model versions, and token-cap differences.
2. **Build a balanced difficulty panel.** Start with `HC-MT-TRIAGE-010`, `HC-MT-MEDSAFE-007`, and `HC-MT-REASON-009`, each in English and Chinese, plus easier non-emergency/negative controls. These are proposed diagnostic items, not approved calibration cases. Add genuinely distinct public-evidence adaptations and near-miss reasoning cases without duplicating a source mechanism across development and future holdout. Do not call public cases “hidden.”
3. **Create response anchors.** For each frozen item, collect independently written excellent, partially correct, omission, and unsafe responses. Write item-specific observable 0–4 anchors for safety, required content, and revision; include acceptable alternatives and explicit critical-error examples. Source-to-label review is needed before any anchor is treated as clinically authoritative.
4. **Measure judge behavior.** Blind both judges to candidate identity, compare each against the same human-adjudicated anchors when available, measure exact agreement and disagreement by category, language and same-vendor pairing, and check whether repeat judgments change. Inspect 100-point ceiling frequency, unsafe false passes, and severity-gate consistency before looking at an overall average. Two AI judges do not substitute for clinical ground truth.
5. **Revise only for validity.** If an item is too easy because it does not demand the stated reasoning, improve its scenario, required behaviors, or discriminating update. If a judge gives 4/4 to a response that misses an explicit requirement, clarify the evidence-backed anchor or judge instruction. Preserve clinically correct alternative approaches. Version changed cases/rubrics and rerun all compared models. Never silently rescore old reports or optimize on the final holdout.
6. **Estimate uncertainty.** Compare score distributions, not a single case or a single mean. Report per-domain and per-language results, ceiling/floor rates, hard-fail rates, judge disagreement, and uncertainty intervals after an adequately sized, prospectively frozen sample. A tier gap is useful only if it persists on untouched cases and does not come from systematic judge bias. External benchmark claims remain gated on independent clinical and language review.

The [OpenAI evaluation guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evaluation-best-practices) recommends difficult and edge-case examples, while its [grader guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/graders) calls for testing graders against varied responses and human ground truth. We use these as methodology guidance, not an OpenAI-hosted evaluation service.
