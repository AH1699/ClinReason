# PubMed as a future case source — intake decision, 2026-10-21

**Decision: yes for discovery and traceable evidence, not as a blanket license or answer key.** PubMed indexes citations, abstracts and links to articles; it is not itself a repository of reusable patient records. A published case report may describe a real clinical event, but its account is selected and retrospective, and often reveals the eventual diagnosis. That makes it useful for finding underrepresented mechanisms and distractors, not a ready-made hidden benchmark prompt or a clinically validated reference answer. See [PubMed Help](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/help/) and the [NCBI API overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/develop/api/).

NLM explicitly says it does **not** own the copyright to PubMed abstracts and cannot grant reuse rights. A free-to-read abstract or a “free full text” link is not proof of permission to redistribute or adapt the narrative. [NCBI policy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/about/policies/) and the [PMC copyright notice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/about/copyright/) require checking the article-level rights. The [PMC Open Access Subset](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/openftlist/) is a practical starting filter, but individual licenses still vary. For this project's potentially public/commercial benchmark, prefer explicit CC BY or CC0 articles for adaptation; assess attribution, derivative, and commercial-use conditions on each record. Do not assume a CC BY-NC or ND article permits the intended reuse. For systematic retrieval, use PMC's sanctioned APIs/datasets, not bulk scraping of article pages; [NCBI's E-utilities guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK25497/) also sets API request-rate and identification expectations.

**Intake rule for every proposed published-case family:**

1. Record PMID, PMCID if present, DOI, article URL, publication date, date checked, article type, license URL/text and any correction/retraction signal. Exclude unclear rights from adapted public text; citations alone may still inform topic selection.
2. Record exactly which *clinical mechanism* the paper motivated and which patient details were changed or invented. Do not copy the abstract, case narrative, figures, rare identifying combinations or the reported outcome into a candidate-visible prompt. A public article cannot become genuinely hidden merely by changing wording; group source-related variants together and mark leakage risk.
3. Check privacy independently of license. [ICMJE](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/protection-of-research-participants.html) advises omitting nonessential identifying details and obtaining consent when anonymity is uncertain. Our prompt should be synthetic/de-identified even if the article is public.
4. Source the **reference criteria separately** from an applicable guideline, systematic review or other appropriate evidence, and explicitly note jurisdiction, date, uncertainty and the case report's generalizability limits. A single unusual outcome is not a general treatment rule. Clinical and bilingual reviewers must later approve both the scenario and answer criteria.
5. Store the provenance and license decision with the draft; keep it in public development. Do not label it calibration or hidden Gold, score it as validated, or silently add it to the default test roster.

As a concrete *intake candidate only*, [PMID 41562822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41562822/) links to [PMCID PMC12821482](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12821482/), an acute angle-closure case report whose PMC record lists CC BY 4.0. It could motivate a medication-plus-vision-red-flag mechanism, but we have **not copied or adapted it**, checked consent/clinical label suitability, or added a family. The rare event and apparent medication association must not be turned into a universal causation label from one case report.

**Current outcome:** PubMed is approved as a *candidate source-discovery channel* under this intake policy. No PubMed-derived case was added in S15; the frozen `REASON-015` scoring probe is from an existing guidance-derived synthetic family. The next expansion batch can pilot a small, license-audited, source-linked case-report tranche and a separate guideline-backed reference-answer draft, all still pending professional review.
